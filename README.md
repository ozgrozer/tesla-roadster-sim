# Tesla Roadster Sim

A Three.js stunt simulation of the Oct 15, 2026 Roadster reveal at McGregor, Texas, and a free-roam city to drive it in. It opens in the city, where Optimus walks up to the parked Roadster, the driver's butterfly door swings up, and it gets in and takes the yoke (any drive key skips this); it stays at the wheel from then on. Press **Test site** at the top (or `C`) for the reveal, where the autopilot runs the show until you press any drive key to take over:

1. **The wall.** From the flat start of the platform, climb 21 m and hang upside down at 170° on the 2 G suction fan.
2. **Reverse.** Back down the wall under brake control and stop on the platform where it started.
3. **Fly.** Full power up the wall and over the top without slowing, rear thrusters along the lip, then a push-off with the bottom ones. Roll upright and free-fall with the thrusters idle, then fire full bottom thrust a few metres off the floor (about 3 G) and settle through the vapor cloud. No flips, as at the event.
4. **The lineup.** A long run down the pad on the main thrusters to 180 km/h, then full lift thrust over the parked Cybercab, Cybertruck, Semi, Model Y and Model 3 with a kickflip (a 360° roll, as at the event), the same last-moment landing burn, then brake to a stop.

**The city.** Where the Roadster starts: a 10 × 10 block downtown grid at golden hour on the coast, a beach and a blue sea beyond the ring road (drive straight off the promenade onto the sand, down to the water's edge), with glass towers, brick and stucco blocks (the bricks at their real size), storefronts under canopies, palm boulevards, parks and parking lots, and roofs laid in membrane strips. Every window is its own: set back in its opening (at an angle you see the reveal, the sill lit and the head in shadow) with a room behind it, a floor, a ceiling, walls and a desk or cabinet against the back, lit by its lamp or dark; warm or cool, blinds up or down, and the glass smooth enough to mirror the sky at a glancing angle, each pane at a slightly different one. The streets are worn the way a city's are: darker, polished wheel tracks down every lane, oil drips between them, patches and tar-sealed cracks, a concrete gutter along every kerb that follows it round the corners, drain grates by the corners, manholes out in the lanes, and turn arrows before each stop line showing the moves the lane allows. The low sun throws the whole city's shadows, not just those round the car: a tower's runs two hundred metres down the avenue, so the streets lie mostly in cool shade with shafts of warm sun between the blocks, and every car sits on its own soft shadow. People walk the pavements, a metre or three in from the kerb, each in their own clothes and build; at the corners some wait at the kerb for the walk (the first seconds of the cross street's green, so they are over before it changes) and cross on the stripes, stepping round a car stopped across them, and traffic and Autopilot stop for anyone in the road. Drive at them and they dive out of the way, arms flying up, then hurry back to the pavement: nobody is ever run down. Two lanes each way, a signal on every corner, and live traffic (sedans, hatchbacks, SUVs, cabs, pickups, vans and buses with rounded bodies and bumpers, door shut lines, black B-pillars, glass that reflects the sky, open wheel arches over tyres on alloy or steel rims, the fronts steering through the turns, and bodies that dip under braking, squat as they pull away and lean out of the corners) that keeps its distance (minding the whole of a car crossing in front, not just its middle), signals and changes lanes, stops for red lights and gives way when turning left, taking a gap of four and a half seconds in the oncoming traffic, or going as the light changes and the oncoming traffic stops. Drivers honk at a Roadster that sits in their way or cuts them up, lean on the horn after you hit them (hazards blinking), and move over when you honk at them from behind, though nobody honks until the Roadster has sounded its own horn at the gridlock: the first horn in town is yours. The kerb is felt: each axle thumps up onto the pavement and drops back off it (the car rides up onto it, front wheels first), a wheel over a manhole clunks (ta-dunk, front then rear), and the tyres grip less on a park's grass and less still on the beach, where the sand drags at them and sprays up behind. Driving well scores too, on the stunt score with the flips and landings: a drift, held well sideways at speed, pays for how long it lasted and how far sideways it went once the car straightens up (nothing, if it ends in a crash), and a near miss, flashing past a car close enough to touch without touching it, pays for how fast you went by, double if it was coming the other way. Hit a car and it gets knocked loose; like GTA, nothing on the road takes the wheel away, however hard you hit it: the Roadster bounces off cars, walls and posts and drives on. Landings don't either: however hard it comes down, anything short of its side or roof lands on its wheels, and flying into the side of a building bounces it off. Only coming down on its side or roof tumbles it. Fly up onto a building and you can land and drive on its roof: the stair housings and air-handling units are solid, but the edge is only a low lip, so you can drive straight off a tall roof and come down on a lower one across the alley (or on the street, if you overshoot). Street furniture breaks the way it would: a street light is a breakaway pole that folds over at a walking pace, a signal mast only goes down in a hit at nearly 50 km/h, and trees and palms snap by the size of their trunk. Each one costs the car the energy it took to snap and the momentum of what it carries along, then falls over like a pole hinged at its foot, the signal going dark and a tree shedding its leaves; a car you knock sliding takes them down too. The thrusters work here too, and one block of the central avenue, straight ahead of where you arrive, is gridlocked solid both ways for jumping (the gold blip on the radar). Press `P` in the city for **Autopilot**: the Roadster drives itself to that gridlock on a green wave (every light on its way turns green for it, after the cross street's yellow, and goes back to its cycle once it is through), pulls up behind the last car in the queue, sits there while the light at the far end of the jammed street turns red, leans on the horn, then lifts straight up on its thrusters, flies down the street over the stopped cars and comes down on the open road beyond. It drives like a Roadster: hard off the line, up to about 120 km/h on the straights, braking late and firmly, and taking corners quicker than traffic does. From there it drives itself through traffic, overtaking slower cars when the other lane is clear and keeping its green wave going (the light at the end of each street turns green for it), and whenever it is stuck in a jam again (not just at a red light) it honks and hops over that too, until you touch a drive key. `C` (or **Test site**) goes back to the reveal. Press `L` and night falls on the city: a deep blue sky with stars and a moon low where the sun was, the hills black against it and the sea dark under the moon's glitter path, half or more of the windows lit and the shopfronts brighter, every street light on and throwing a pool of warm light on the street, the traffic's headlight beams on the road and the Roadster's dipped headlights lighting the street ahead; the birds go quiet and the crickets start up. (Cars parked in the lots have their lights off, day or night.)

**The people in it.** Each driver in the city is their own: some dawdle, most keep to about the limit and a fifth press on; they follow at their own distance, pull away and brake at their own rate and react at their own speed, so a queue moves off at a green in a wave, each car a moment after the one ahead. Nobody stamps on the pedals without reason: speed changes smoothly, the brakes come on hard only in an emergency, and the bodies dip, squat and lean on their springs with it (a bus slower and softer). Drivers stop a little short of the line and creep up to it, a bold one goes on through an amber they could have stopped for, nobody pulls into a junction with no room to get out of it, and a car turning left waits for a gap and then goes as the light changes. A lane change is signalled first and eased over on a smooth S; quicker drivers overtake a slow car. Come at a car and its driver stamps on the brakes, swerves and leans on the horn; come down from the air in front of them and everyone ahead panics; hit one and it slides to a stop, sits there with its hazards on (its driver honking if you are still about), then steers back into its lane and drives on, while the cars behind move over to get round it. A car you send sliding into another knocks that one loose too. Two cars stuck nose to nose in a junction sort it out, one backing up (reversing lights on) to let the other by. People walk at their own pace and build, some in pairs, some jogging, some standing about on their phones or chatting; heads turn to follow the Roadster when it goes by loud and fast; now and then someone jaywalks once the traffic is far enough off (it stops for them). Drive on the pavement, crash, land or hit something hard nearby and everyone round about runs screaming along the pavement away from it, then calms down a few seconds later.

The traffic is built the way cars are: headlights in their own lenses and housings, with running lights along their tops by day and the indicators at their outer ends, red tail clusters with their indicators and reversing lamps (brighter as the car brakes, white as it backs up), a high stop lamp on the hatches, SUVs, vans and buses, repeaters on the front wings, a lit sign on every cab and a route board on every bus; body-coloured bumpers over a dark plastic valance (and dark cladding along an SUV's or pickup's sills), body-coloured wing mirrors, framed windows, number plates front and back, and paint under a hard clear coat with the sky in it. Buses have their windows down the side between pillars, a windscreen and a lit route board. People are shaped rather than boxed, and each dresses their own way: a jacket or bare arms, trousers, shorts or a dress, long hair or short, a bag or a backpack, dark shoes or white trainers.

Fan-made, built from press coverage. Not affiliated with Tesla or SpaceX.

## Run it

**Double-click `index.html`.** It works offline and needs no server: three.js and the fonts are in `vendor/`, and the game code is bundled into one plain script, `dist/roadster.js`. Add `#site` to the address to start at the test site.

A local server still works if you prefer one: `npm start`, or double-click `start.command` on macOS.

## Controls

| Key | On the ground / wall | In the air |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W / S | Throttle / brake, squeezed on over a third of a second (a tap is a light brake), then held at a stop and reverse after half a second (also back down the wall); both together at a crawl, a burnout (steer for donuts) | Pitch down / up |
| A / D | Steer; after a crash, roll it back onto its wheels | Roll |
| Q / E | — | Yaw |
| Space | E-brake: with the wheel straight, an emergency stop on all four wheels (each held at its grip by the ABS, as a Tesla's Park button held at speed), biting in a twentieth of a second and as hard as the tyres allow (about 11 m from 60 km/h, 30 m from 100), whatever W is doing; turned, it locks only the rears, which step straight out for GTA-style handbrake turns, and at speed it puts the car in Track Mode for drifting | — |
| Shift | Main thrusters (boost) | Main thrusters |
| F | Lift thrusters (fly, take off) | Lift thrusters |
| H | Horn (hold) | Horn |
| X / K | — | Auto backflip / kickflip |
| G | Suction fan: auto, on, off | |

**Gamepad** (any with the standard layout: Xbox, PlayStation and most others): the left stick steers (and in the air pitches and rolls), `RT` is an analog throttle, `LT` an analog brake and then reverse, `A` the e-brake, `RB` boost, `LB` fly, `X` the horn, `Y` the camera, the D-pad up and down a backflip and a kickflip, `View` reset and `Menu` Autopilot. The right stick looks round the car (and yaws it in the air), and the pad rumbles for knocks, kerbs, landings and crashes. A stick held over through a lift-off only flies the car once it has come back to the middle.

Other keys: `C` city / test site, `L` night in the city (and back to golden hour), `P` autopilot demo (Autopilot in the city), `M` manual, `R` reset, `1` `2` `3` start line / wall top / lineup, `V` camera, `T` slow-mo, `N` sound, `I` hide the key panel. Release the throttle and the car coasts down on regen, like a Tesla. S squeezes the brake pedal rather than stamping on it: the brakes come on over about a third of a second, about how long a real hard stop takes to bite, so a tap takes a few km/h off and holding it stops the car from 100 km/h in about 33 m (a good performance car's distance); held through the stop, it keeps the car on its brakes for half a second, as a driver stops and then selects reverse, before it backs up (pressed again at a standstill, it backs up at once). The brakes are shared out by the load on each axle, as electronic brake-force distribution does, with ABS holding the tyres at their peak: the tyres howl and the HUD shows ABS while it works. Brake hard in a corner at the limit and the stability control lets the light rears off their brakes and regen, so the car runs a touch wide rather than swinging its tail round (the HUD shows ESC). The steering keys ease the wheel over rather than throwing it to full lock: at a standstill full lock comes in a tenth of a second, at 100 km/h in about a third of a second (and no slower than four tenths however fast the car goes, as the steering's reach is small up there), so at speed a quick tap is a small correction (a lane's worth over three seconds), holding the key a lane change and then a turn at the tyres' limit; let go and the wheel comes back two and a half times as fast (with the handbrake pulled, or the car sliding, the keys are as quick as at a standstill). Underneath, the steering is a rack turned at the speed of a driver's hands (full lock in about a third of a second) that comes back to the middle on its own, quicker the faster the car rolls. Each tyre builds grip with its slip angle up to a peak, about 11° on the fronts, and gives less past it, down to the 80% a sliding tyre keeps; the steering stops just short of that peak, so the car corners at up to about 1.2 g, the grip of its tyres. In a slide the front wheels follow the slide, as a real car's do through their caster, and the keys steer from there: left alone they let the slide run out (with a touch of counter-steer catching the car as it swings further round), the way into the turn holds it and the other way catches it. A tap of Space on full lock swings the car round on its locked rears, GTA-style: pulled with the wheel turned, the rears step straight out rather than waiting for the fronts to drag the car round (about 90° in eight tenths of a second at 50 km/h, a half-second tap at 60 km/h taking it about 90° round, a right-angle turn). Let go and get on the power and it drives off the way it points; keep the wheel turned and it carries on in a power slide. Off the handbrake, a car turning faster than its tyres could carry it round is caught, as a driver's hands would catch it, so a handbrake turn stops swinging soon after Space is let go instead of spinning on round: hold it to a hundred and eighty degrees and let go, and it drives off back the way it came. `W` and `S` together at a crawl are a **burnout**: the fronts hold the car on their brakes while the rears spin against them in a cloud of smoke and a scream of rubber, the tail squatting; turn the wheel and the rears swing out round the fronts in a **donut**, about a turn every three seconds, each full turn scoring (more for every one on top). Let go of `S` and it leaves on its spinning rears. Pulled at speed, Space also puts the car in **Track Mode**, as in a Tesla's Track Mode with its balance set to the rear: traction and stability control stand back, the drive goes to the rears, and the two rear motors share the torque out between them to hold the drift at the angle you ask for. Hold `W` after the flick and the car drifts; steer into the turn (`A` in a left-hand drift) to hold it or take it deeper (about 50°), tap it for a shallower drift, leave the wheel alone and it unwinds and drives on (a small flick is a short slide, a big one a long drift), and lift off or tap the other way and it straightens in a moment, without the tail snapping round. Held in a drift, `W` keeps the pace the drift began at rather than piling on speed, as a drifter feathers the throttle, so the tyres carry the car round the corner instead of out wide: from 50 km/h it comes round a right angle in about 30 m, and held on it circles at a steady pace about 27 m across (`S` mid-drift brings the pace down, and the drift goes on at it). Hold the other way with `W` on and the car swings through straight into a drift that way, a **linked drift**, which scores as one with half as much again for each link. Once the car has run straight again the electronics are back. The follow camera swings round to the outside of a drift, so you see the car side on. Above 100 km/h the active rear wing rises out of the deck on its swan-neck pylons (it stows again below 60), and tips up as an air brake under hard braking. The body pitches and rolls on its springs: it dives about two and a half degrees under full braking, squats as it launches and leans about four degrees at the tyres' limit, settling with a touch of overshoot. Knocks leave their mark, like GTA: anything harder than about 9 km/h dents the panels where it landed, deeper and wider the harder the hit, the metal crumpled and catching the light, and a crash crumples whichever side it lands on; the dents add up (to a limit) until the car is reset. After a crash the wreck stays where it stops, like GTA: on its roof or side, `A` or `D` rolls it back onto its wheels once it has slowed, and once it is on them any drive key carries on, even while it is still sliding (`R` still resets). Keys still held when the car leaves the ground (W for the run-up, A/D) don't fly it until you press them again, so holding W through a jump doesn't pitch the nose down.

The sound is all synthesised, modelled on what makes the real thing. The Roadster's three motors sing with their gear-mesh and electromagnetic orders (a whine locked to the motors' speed, swelling through the housing's resonances, louder under power and changing flank on regen), the two rear motors beat against each other in a corner and stop with the wheels on the e-brake, the inverter's tones step and split apart at walking pace, the gears' lash clunks as the torque reverses, and below 30 km/h it hums its pedestrian warning. The tyres roar with speed and with what is under them (smoother on the pavement with a "ta-dum" at each slab joint, a swish on grass, a hiss of grains on sand), sing as they near their grip and scream past it, chattering, front and rear each in its own voice; the locked rears of the e-brake scrub and howl, a burnout screams in place with the rear motors revving, and on grass or sand the tyres only scrub. The wind rises steeply with speed, gusting and buffeting. From the follow camera the car is heard outside, late by the time its sound takes to arrive (so it has its doppler on a fly-by); from the cockpit the street is muffled through the canopy. Knocks are layered by how hard and what was hit (a thud, a bumper cracking, steel buckling, glass going; heavier with grit against a wall, a ringing post, a splitting trunk and thrashing leaves), grinding along a wall scrapes for as long as it lasts, and the wreck grinds along on its roof. Traffic is five voices for the nearest cars: petrol engines with automatic gearboxes (idling at the lights, revving as they pull away, shifting up, engine braking), a V8 pickup's burble, diesel vans and buses with their turbos, hybrid taxis, electric cars' whine and warning hum, every car's tyres, with doppler, fading and dulling with distance, and muffled when a building is in the way; the gridlock idles, a car backing up whines in reverse, a startled driver's tyres squeal, and cars knocked into each other crunch where they hit. Horns are taps, double toots or long angry blasts; buses squeal their brakes and let their air brakes go as they stop; a parked car knocked loose sets its alarm off. The city round you: far traffic on either side, wind between the buildings, the surf and gulls by the beach, birds by day and crickets at night, a siren across town, a dog, building work, the murmur and footsteps of people close by, and their screams as they run from a fright. The street canyon's reverb is long and dense between tall buildings and thin out in the open, and your own sound comes back off the walls either side.

Smoke is carried by the air rather than flung: thruster vapor, tyre smoke and dust drift on a light breeze, curl and billow in eddies, get dragged along in the car's wake, and are lit by the low sun (they glow at the edges when you look towards it). Hot tyre smoke climbs as it trails behind a slide; the thrusters' cold vapor sinks and spreads across the ground in a cloud.

In the city a radar at the bottom left turns with the camera, zooming out with speed; traffic shows as dots and wrecks in amber. The follow camera pulls in rather than sit inside a building, and when a knock spins the car or sends it sliding, it follows where the car is going rather than whirl round its nose. It gives with the car: it falls back as the car launches and closes in under hard braking, the road trembles up through it at speed (coarser on sand and grass), and past about 150 km/h, or with the main thrusters firing, the edges of the picture streak in towards the car.

**Performance.** The frame is drawn at the screen's full pixel density (up to 2× on a Retina screen) while the GPU keeps up, and at less when it doesn't: the game times the GPU's own work on each frame (where the browser allows it, otherwise it goes by the frame rate, and by how long its own physics, traffic and drawing calls took), steps the resolution down below about 54 frames a second (further at once when frames are well behind) and back up once there is room, so slower graphics get a steady frame rate rather than a stutter, while a slow processor, which fewer pixels wouldn't help, keeps a sharp picture. Every step is checked: one down that doesn't make the frames shorter is undone, so a 50 Hz screen, or a browser holding the page to 30 frames a second (to save battery, or in a frame on another site), doesn't cost sharpness, and one up that doesn't hold goes back to where it was and is tried again later. A change always waits for the start of a frame, so it never flashes black. The city is drawn from what hides the most to what hides the least (the car, the buildings, the street furniture and the traffic, then the pavements, the asphalt, the beach and the sea, and the sky last, only where nothing else is), so the graphics card never shades ground or sky that something stands in front of. The street furniture, cornices and roofs are split into nine districts, so the car's own shadow map, sixty metres across, and the view leave out the districts they can't see. Only the cars and people the camera can see (or whose shadows it can) are drawn, which halves the triangles in a frame; windows too far off to see into skip their rooms, and the wall between them skips the window work; the city's shadows are drawn once into their own map, and read with four taps rather than sixteen; the bloom is worked out at the window's size rather than the screen's pixels, and added in the same last pass that blurs, tone maps and writes the frame; and the canvas doesn't keep a multisampled buffer it would never use. The Roadster's fixed pieces are baked into a few buffers, so it draws in half the calls (and its cabin, whose shadow falls inside its own, stays out of the shadow map). The HUD writes only what has changed, and the city's textures are drawn straight into memory at startup. While the intro plays, every shader the game can need (the city by day and by night, the test site, the thrusters' jets and the speed blur) is compiled ahead, so the first night, the first trip to the test site and the first thruster burst don't stall. Only the cars and people within 45 m of the Roadster are drawn into its shadow map (the map is sixty metres across, so nothing it shows changes), cars further off ride on plainer wheels, and the traffic and the people allocate next to nothing per frame, so the garbage collector has little to do.

Cameras (`V`): **Director** (the autopilot's shots), **Follow** (GTA-style: sits close behind the car's heading, about six metres back and a little above, so the car fills the lower middle of the picture; it opens out a little and looks further down the road as the speed builds, ignores flips; drag with the mouse to look around, scroll to zoom), **Chase**, **Cockpit** (from the driver's seat, through the bronze tint of the canopy: the yoke turns with the steering, Optimus's hands going round with it, the touchscreen on the dash shows the speed, the drive selector, power and regen and a map of the streets round the car, the view rides on the springs and tilts with the car, and the street is heard muffled through the glass; drag to look around), **Side**, **Free** (orbit).

## Editing the code

The page runs the bundle, so rebuild after changing anything in `js/`:

```
npm run build     # once
npm run watch     # rebuild on every save
```

No dependencies to install: the bundler is a single file with no packages. `npm test` fails if `dist/roadster.js` is out of date.

`npm run og` renders `og-image.png`, the 1200 × 630 link preview, from the game itself in headless Chrome (it needs Chrome and Node 22; set `CHROME` to use another browser binary). The page's Open Graph tags point at the copy on GitHub Pages.

## Tests

```
npm test
```

These run the full autopilot sequence, plus coast-down, braking, e-brake, drift, stability crash, crash-recovery, landing and lineup-bump checks, headless in Node against the same sim code the browser uses. The city tests check that the lane graph joins up, that traffic never runs a red light or drives through another car, that the Roadster bounces off buildings and cars however hard it hits them (on the road or flying), and shunts cars, that Autopilot gets round town cleanly, that no traffic drives into the jammed street and a jump clears it, that Autopilot drives to the gridlock, joins the back of the queue clear of the junction, honks and flies over it without touching anything, that no driver honks before the Roadster has honked at the gridlock, that the Roadster can land on a roof and drive straight off its edge onto a lower one, that signal masts, street lights, trees and palms stand up to a slow knock and snap in a hard one (at a cost in speed, and to a sliding car too), and that random driving never ends in NaN. Street tests check that the kerb runs along each street and round each corner, which surface a point is on, that the tyres grip less and drag more on sand, and that the Roadster feels each axle mount the kerb and each wheel run over a manhole. Track Mode tests check that a drift is held for as long as the steering asks, unwinds and hands back to the electronics with the wheel left alone, and ends at once with a tap out of it or lifting off. Drift tests drive it through the keyboard as a player does: that Space with the wheel straight bites at once and stops from 100 km/h in under 31 m, W held or not, while on the turn it swings the car round; that a flick of Space on the turn starts a drift W and the wheel hold steadily from 40 to 130 km/h without spinning, that a held drift keeps its pace and comes round a right angle in a corner's length and circles tight, that holding the turn drifts deeper than tapping it, that lifting off, tapping the other way or steering out off the power straighten it without a snap or a flick the other way, that holding the other way on the power links it into a drift that way which unwinds as gently as the first and scores as one, and that braking mid-drift brings its pace down; input tests that the steering keys ease the wheel over gently at speed and at once with the handbrake. Stunt tests check that a held drift scores once it straightens up (and one into a crash doesn't), and that passing a car close at speed is a near miss while passing it a lane away is not. Pedestrian tests check that people keep to the pavement, cross only on the walk, are never run into by traffic or left stuck halfway over, dive out of the Roadster's way so that nobody is ever under it, and that Autopilot stops for someone in the road. Input tests check that W held through a lift-off doesn't pitch the car into the ground, and the key map (Space the e-brake, F the lift thrusters, H the horn), and that a gamepad's triggers and stick are analog throttle, brake and steering, and a stick held through a lift-off doesn't roll the car. Braking tests check that a tap of S is a light brake and holding it a full stop in a real car's distance, that S held through a stop holds the car before it backs up, that braking hard in a corner at the limit at 50, 90 and 120 km/h never slides the tail out past the tyres' peak, that the ABS works only when the brakes ask for more than the tyres have, and that the e-brake bites in a twentieth of a second, holding the rears at their peak with the wheel straight and locking them on the turn. Handling tests check that the e-brake with the wheel straight stops the car about as short as the brakes and straight, W held or not, that a handbrake turn swings the car round and its slide runs out no harder than the tyres can pull (nothing snaps it onto its new heading) before it drives off the way it points, that the steering turns at the speed of hands, that the car corners at the grip of its tyres, and that the tyre curve peaks and falls away to a sliding tyre's grip. One more test checks that the bundle matches the sources. Burnout tests check that W and S together at a crawl hold the car while the rears spin, that steered it spins round on the spot and scores each donut, and that it drives off straight once S is let go. Traffic tests check that drivers differ, that a queue moves off in a wave, that a 300 s run has no car through another, no late red-light run, no instant change of speed, no snap in heading and no car stuck for good, that a car the Roadster comes at brakes hard, swerves and honks, that a knocked car sits with its hazards on and then steers back into its lane, and that the Roadster landing in front makes drivers brake; car-looks tests check that every type has its lamps, where they belong, within its triangle budget; and the people's tests also count jaywalkers, pairs, idlers and joggers, and check that the Roadster on the pavement sends people running, screaming, and that they calm down. Give-way tests check that Autopilot doesn't wait for ever to turn left across traffic, that people waiting to cross stand clear of the road, that cars pull out round the stopped Roadster and edge round a wreck, that a reset in mid-air sets off no panic, and that a car stopped with its nose over the line is driven round.
